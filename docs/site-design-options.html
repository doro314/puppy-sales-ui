Revised after your review. The first version of this table ranked parents and health as a one-time read. They're the trust step every buyer takes before reaching out, so the litter details belong on the first screen — today the problem is their shape, not their place.

  1. Home has the right facts in the wrong shape. On a phone the first screen is the site name, “Home” twice, a $100 banner and nine equal cells — the birth date weighs the same as “Vaccinations ——” — and the first puppy photo is at y 906, below the screen. The parents are text in a cell: “Coco / Havapoo”. On desktop the first photo starts at y 701 of 800.
  2. No page shows the puppies together. Comparing five means five trips through the menu — two taps each on a phone, with the menu covering the page while it's open.
  3. Availability is invisible until you've clicked in. The menu shows names and sex. Two of five are reserved, and a visitor finds that out on arrival. As a litter sells, more taps end at “Reserved”.
  4. Sex is said four times; status once. The title color, the card's top bar, a pink pill in the first cell and the menu paw all say girl. The thing that changes week to week — status — is a small badge at the bottom of the card.
  5. The inquiry is the last thing on the page. “Inquire About Bella” is at y 1260 on a phone, after ten thumbnails, and at y 1148 on desktop. The only other way to inquire is Home's banner, which comes before any puppy.
  6. Photos are small and don't swipe. 280 px tall on a phone — shorter than the facts card above it — with two 36 px buttons below the frame and no swipe. Square photos get blurred bars down each side.
  7. The phone page is heavy, and pinned. 7.9 MB of photos for Bella. And the page is locked to the screen height with only the content pane scrolling, so 128 px of header never scroll away, Safari's toolbars never collapse, and tapping the status bar doesn't scroll to the top.
  8. Nothing can be shared. Every puppy lives at the same address: you can't text a link to Olive, a refresh goes back to Home, and Back leaves the site.
  9. Grey text fails contrast. The grey used for descriptions, facts and sub-copy measures 2.84:1 on white, labels 2.25:1, white on the blue button 3.34:1 and the orange banner 2.15–2.99:1. WCAG AA asks for 4.5:1. §05 computes every pair.
  10. When a litter sells out, the site keeps selling. Once every puppy is reserved or adopted, puppy pages show no button at all, and Home still says “Reserve today — $100 holds your pup”.

Build order — both docs, one sequence

SRC, LIT, IMG, DATA and DFIX are the data doc's codes; the rest are this doc's. The work happens on a branch, because a push to main goes live: steps 1–3 can go live as they land, since visitors see nothing change but lighter photos, and the redesign goes live as one merge once it's measured.

  1. DATA-1 with the data doc's §07 files. The schema, the converted litters.json and the checker, wired into build, dev and test, while the pages still read puppies.jsx.
  2. SRC-2 + LIT-2, with DFIX-4. The site reads litters.json and the photo folders move. Every page looks as it does now apart from casing fixes — the proof that the conversion is right.
  3. IMG-1, with DFIX-2. Alone, so a visual regression is easy to bisect.
  4. PLUS-1 links, then PLUS-4 one bar. Links need step 2's slugs. The bar moves every y on the phone, so it lands before anything is measured — and alone, so the iPhone retest is about one thing.
  5. DATA-2, -3, -4. The words and numbers the new pages show: “White & black parti”, “27.8 oz at 3 weeks”, the cover photo.
  6. PLUS-8's litter card and tiles, then HOME-8, then PUP-2, with PLUS-2 and PLUS-3, which they're drawn with. The card goes first because Home, the puppy pages and every season reuse it. Gallery and carousel photos after the first load lazily — otherwise Home's 27 download at once and the 0.27 MB row misses.
  7. PLUS-5 and PLUS-6. The seasons, and the inquiry that knows the puppy — checked with one real test inquiry.
  8. FIX-1 … FIX-7 alongside — FIX-1 first if anything ships early, since it touches every page.
  9. DATA-5 and DFIX-1. The owner's manual describes what was actually built.
  10. Re-measure against the table above, plus one run per season — expecting, sold out, two litters — from fixture files, and report the misses.
  11. The tutorial. Once everything is built: a plain-language guide to managing litters, docs/data-tutorial.html, with a small working editor that checks what you type the way the build does.

Then, when the next litter's details arrive, it goes in as a new litter and the June five become "adopted" — the owner's manual's first real use.

Before you start — four ways this ships broken. A push to main goes live, so the work happens on a branch. · The Mac ignores the case of file names and Cloudflare's Linux build doesn't: the checker compares names with each folder's real listing, and skips the .DS_Store in every photo folder. · ?parents=true shows /Coco.jpg and /Leo.jpg from public/, and step 2 moves those files — decide the flag before step 2. · The 16 tests mock today's data shape: each step that replaces a component replaces its tests, and each season gets a fixture file the tests render.
The one thing I'd flag: PUP-2 drops the thumbnail grid on phones. If people mostly scan a puppy's photos at a glance rather than swipe through them, keep a single row of small thumbnails under the photo: it costs about 70 px and puts Inquire at roughly y 634 — still inside the first screen. That reverses independently of everything else.
Needed before building, not before deciding: the wording of the waitlist message · whether to ask for a phone number at all · whether the sold-out June litter simply disappears when the new one goes up, or stays below it as “every puppy has a home” · whether ?parents=true stays, reading the parents from the data, or goes — before step 2 · whether Home's gallery keeps autoplaying every 8 s as today; I'd keep it, paused while it's touched and for anyone with reduced motion turned on · whether the $100 deposit can differ by litter; if so, it becomes a litter field instead of a number in the code.
Not drawn yet: Home with two litters listed — it happens if the June litter stays, or if a puppy is still unsold when the next litter is born. I'd lead with the newest litter as drawn and follow it with a short section for the older one: its card line and its puppies, no second gallery. And where “More about the litter” goes: I'd open it in place on Home, with a puppy page's “About the litter ›” landing on Home with it open — no new page.
